Making local restroom information more useful

Reliable discovery starts with separating what is known about a place from what is known about its restroom. A listing can identify a real business while its restroom status remains unknown. Helpful contributions close that gap without overstating certainty.

Report what you actually know

For a recent visit, describe the conditions you observed: available restroom types, whether the facilities seemed usable, relevant cleanliness details, and any access requirements. Avoid guessing about facilities you did not see. If you didn't enter the restroom, don't mark its condition as personally verified.

Separate firsthand visits from prior knowledge

You may remember that a location has a changing table or requires customers to ask for a key. Older knowledge is still useful when presented honestly with an approximate timeframe. Make clear when a report is from memory, rather than representing it as a new onsite check-in.

Use detail that changes a visitor's decision

  • Access: public, customer-only, key or code required, or uncertain.
  • Facilities: family, accessible or other restroom types, if actually observed.
  • Condition: specific issues such as missing soap, unavailable stalls, or a closed restroom, where relevant.
  • Timing: when you saw it, and whether conditions might be temporary.

A numerical score alone cannot explain why a location works for one visitor and not another. Specific notes improve the usefulness of ratings.

Keep photos and comments respectful

Do not photograph other visitors, children, private activities, or identifying personal details in a restroom. Avoid posting staff names or accusations as fact. Photos of facilities should respect privacy, business rules and applicable law. If you cannot document something safely, a factual written observation is better.

Correct outdated or incomplete information

Businesses renovate, change hours and access policies, and occasionally close. If an earlier report seems wrong, provide a new observation with context rather than assuming the earlier contributor acted in bad faith. Unknown data should remain marked as unknown until there is evidence to update it.

Good evidence builds trust: a clear source, reasonable timeframe and a specific observation are more useful than an unsupported claim of certainty.

Find a place to contribute to

Open the web app, choose a place on the map or results list, and inspect its location details. Contributions and personal activity may require sign-in or location permissions. You can still use public discovery and read these guides without an account.
